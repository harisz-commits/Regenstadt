Repaint this into the INTERIOR of a municipal records office at night — the
public counter of a registry where deaths are certified. Photoreal, cinematic,
neo-noir.

KEEP from the reference: the camera height, the horizontal line where floor
meets far wall, and the centred vanishing point. The room recedes straight
ahead exactly as the reference does.

THE ROOM: worn parquet-pattern linoleum, walls in institutional ochre gone
grey. Dead centre in the far wall, a wooden counter with a glass screen above
it and a narrow gap beneath. Behind that glass sits ONE man, late fifties, in a
buttoned grey cardigan, a desk lamp pulled low over his papers, a rubber stamp
and an ink pad beside his right hand. He has looked up and is watching the
camera, pen still held. On the LEFT, a wall of small wooden card-index drawers
with brass handles, floor to shoulder height, one drawer standing open. On the
RIGHT, a heavy double door with a small square window, closed, colder light
behind it.

The left card drawers and the right door must each read clearly as a way to go.

LIGHT: one warm desk lamp behind the glass, weak greenish ceiling tubes
elsewhere, and cold blue leaking through the right-hand door window. Long
shadows, dust in the light. No rain indoors.

MOOD: a place where a signature turns a person into a number.

LOOK: photorealistic cinematic film still, anamorphic 35mm, Blade Runner 2049.
Fine film grain. Highlights roll off, never clip to white.

FORBIDDEN: no other people, no readable text, no watermark, no logo, no
readable labels on the drawers. Not an illustration, not anime.
